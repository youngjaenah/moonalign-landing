How it works

From planning to on-site checks to the shot

Plan the spot and framing in advance, then check the real view while scouting and on the day.

1

At home · plan on the Mac

Plan

Pick a target and a date, and the shooting spots where the Moon lines up with the target appear on the map as a band. The app works out the angles, heights, and distances.

AR Check

Scouting and the day

Checking again on site

Trees, cranes, and fences that aren’t on any map have to be checked on site. Look at the real view with a scouting photo and AR.

Check the view with a scouting photo

Load a scouting photo with shooting data (EXIF), and the Moon’s position and path at the planned time are drawn on the photo.

  • A sky grid for azimuth and altitude
  • Check the Moon’s position and path with the time slider
  • Uses the photo’s capture time, focal length, and location
  • Enter the camera clock error to correct the capture time

Check the direction with AR

Hold up your iPhone or iPad on site and the Moon’s path appears over the camera view. Set up the tripod and check the direction before the Moon rises.

  • Paths of the Moon, the ISS, and the galactic core
  • Correct compass error with Align
  • See the whole path while the body is up
  • Available on iPhone and iPad
Modes

A mode for each subject

From a structure lined up with the Moon to the moment the ISS or a plane crosses it.

Structure

Plan framings where a tower, bridge, or skyline lines up with the Moon. Heights of major landmarks are filled in automatically.

Person

Plan a silhouette against the Moon. Shooting spots are shown for a person 1.7 m tall.

ISS

From NASA orbit data, predicts where and when the ISS crosses the Moon or Sun, to the second. Search again the day before you shoot.

Plane

Checks live flight paths for planes likely to cross the Moon and alerts you at the expected moment, so you’re ready to burst and don’t miss it.

Milky Way

When the galactic core is visible, and the shooting spot’s estimated light pollution class.

Anywhere in the world

Plan with worldwide terrain. 3D buildings come from V-World in Korea and Google Photorealistic 3D elsewhere.

More tools

For before and after the shot

  • Widgets — today’s Moon and Sun and your next shooting chance on the Home Screen and Lock Screen.
  • Add to Calendar — save plans and sky events as calendar events with reminders.
  • Landmarks — plan with landmarks whose heights are on file, and save places you measured yourself.

How to

How to use each mode

Follow the key steps to plan a shot. Every screen and setting is explained in the user guide.

AStructure — the Moon lined up with a tower

Choose a target, pick a shooting spot on the map, and check the framing.

  1. Set the target.In a new project, choose ‘Structure’ as the subject and set the place you want to shoot as the target.
  2. Choose the shooting spot.The yellow band lines up at moonrise, the blue band at moonset. Set an accessible spot with a clear view as the shooting spot.
  3. Check the date and framing.Search for alignment dates, then adjust the time and lens focal length in the simulation to check the framing.
  4. Save the plan and check the site.Before the shoot, check the real view with a scouting photo or AR.

BPerson — a silhouette in front of the Moon

Stand far from the person and zoom in to keep them the same size, and the Moon looks larger by comparison.

  1. Choose where the person stands.Choose ‘Person’ as the subject and set a spot with sky behind it, such as a ridge, as the target.
  2. Adjust the shooting spot and height.Pick a shooting spot on the band. A lower camera shows more sky behind the person.
  3. Check framing and focus.Adjust the focal length and aperture in the simulation, and check that both the person and the Moon can be sharp.
  4. Fine-tune on site.Check the real view and adjust your position and framing as the Moon moves.

CISS — the moment it crosses the Moon

Check the spot and time in advance so you can catch the brief crossing.

  1. Find transit areas.Choose ‘ISS’ as the subject, move the map to your area, and tap ‘Find transit spots’.
  2. Check the spot and transit time.Set a shooting spot on a predicted band and use ‘Search 14 days’ to see the transit time and path there.
  3. Prepare the framing.Select and save a prediction, then step through the ISS path in 0.1-second increments in the simulation.
  4. Search again before the shoot.Predicted times can change when the ISS orbit is adjusted. Check again with the latest data the day before.

DPlane — live crossing alerts

Check likely crossings from live flight paths and get ready to shoot.

  1. Set the shooting spot.Choose ‘Plane’ as the subject and set where you will shoot.
  2. Start live watch.Start ‘Live watch’ and check nearby aircraft’s expected crossing times and how close they come to the Moon.
  3. Shoot on the alert.Watch for the crossing alert and countdown, and get ready to burst.
  4. Connect a receiver if you have one.With your own ADS-B receiver, data updates every second.
Google 3D key

Photorealistic 3D abroad — free with your own Google key

3D buildings in Korea come from V-World with no key needed. Photorealistic 3D buildings abroad come from the Google Maps API, and Google charges each time it is used. The overseas 3D subscription exists to cover that fee — it isn’t there to make extra money. Register your own Google API key and you can use exactly the same thing without a subscription.

How to create and set up a Google API key →

Your own key takes priority over the subscription. It’s stored in iCloud Keychain on Apple devices and on the device on Android, sent only to Google, and never to the developer.

Typical planning use stays mostly within Google’s monthly free allowance. Beyond it, Google’s pricing applies, so keep an eye on usage and charges in the Google Cloud console.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

How accurate is it?

The error in the sky position calculation itself is very small. Most of the error in real results comes from terrain data and height inputs. Terrain data can be off by a few meters to tens of meters depending on the place, so the Moon may appear later than shown over a forested ridge, and with a narrow alignment such as Person mode, a few meters can change the result. For an important shoot, check again with the simulation and with Photo Check and AR Check on site.

Why should I search ISS predictions again?

The station adjusts its orbit from time to time. Predictions use NASA’s published orbit data (OEM), and each data set is valid for a limited period. Search again the day before the shoot so the latest orbit data is used.

Where does the live plane data come from?

By default, adsb.lol community data (ODbL 1.0), updated every 30 seconds and stopping automatically after 2 hours. If you run your own ADS-B receiver (readsb/dump1090), enter its LAN address for 1-second updates with no time limit.

Can I use it outside Korea?

Yes. Shooting spots and times are calculated from worldwide terrain data. The 3D simulation uses V-World in Korea; elsewhere it shows photorealistic 3D buildings with a subscription or your own Google API key. Without either, you still see the Moon’s path and the terrain.

Which devices and languages are supported?

On iPhone, iPad, and Mac (macOS 14 or later), one App Store purchase covers all of them, and projects sync through iCloud. On Android (Android 12 or later), buy it separately on Google Play; projects sync between Android devices through Google Drive. Between Apple devices and Android, share plans with a link. AR Check needs a camera, so it isn’t available on the Mac. Languages are English, Korean, and Japanese; the app follows your system setting or can be changed in the app.

Why is overseas 3D a subscription? Can I use it without one?

Overseas photorealistic 3D buildings come from the Google Maps API, and Google charges each time it is used. The subscription has the app pay that fee for you — it isn’t there to make extra money.
